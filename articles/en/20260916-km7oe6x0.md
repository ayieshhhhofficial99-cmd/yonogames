<h2>Body</h2>
<p>Watch: What does the munitions shortage mean for the US?</p>
<p>The US defence department's inspector general says the war with Iran has led to "shortfalls" in munitions and a bottleneck in resupply, contradicting President Donald Trump who has repeatedly denied the US is facing shortages.</p>
<p>Trump has claimed US munitions supplies are "virtually unlimited" and said reporting otherwise is "fake news".</p>
<p>But the independent watchdog said the US spent more than $22 billion on munitions from February through to June, leading to "strategic inventory shortfalls".</p>
<p>When asked about the watchdog's report, the White House said the US had "more than enough" munitions.</p>
<p>The report provides a first-of-its-kind, independent glimpse into US weapons stockpiles, data that is typically classified.</p>
<p>Previous estimates have painted a dire picture of the US shortages.</p>
<p>Over the course of the war, US forces have expended vast quantities of both long-range ground attack systems and the air and missile defences used to intercept incoming Iranian attacks.</p>
<p>A late July estimate from the Center for Strategic and International Studies, or CSIS, found that stocks of some interceptors - such as the Patriot missile and Terminal High Altitude Area Defense, or THAAD - were significantly depleted, in some cases by more than half.</p>
<p>The inspector general's report did not disclose exactly how many of the weapons remain.</p>
<p>It did, however, note the inventory shortfalls and reveal "industrial base bottlenecks" for munitions resupply.</p>
<p>The report added that the defence department is now working to "streamline procurement processes and production lead times" to address the shortages and "respond rapidly to a contingency".</p>
<p>The White House has rejected the watchdog's assessment.</p>
<p>"The United States military has more than enough munitions, ammo, and stockpiles to serve all of the Commander-in-Chief's strategic goals and beyond," spokeswoman Anna Kelly said in a statement on Tuesday.</p>
<p>Trump and other administration officials have also sought to cast reports of shortages as false and "treasonous" .</p>
<p>In a 4 August X post, Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth described news reporting on the shortages as "not true".</p>
<p>And in a lengthy Truth Social post on 3 September, Trump claimed that the US had "virtually unlimited" amounts of ammunition and is "stockpiling and preparing for any contingency that could happen".</p>
<p>The watchdog's report also notes that equipment losses have totalled $3.7bn, with another $7.4bn in other expenditures. Overall, the US spent $33.4bn (£24.7bn) on the war from 28 Feb to 30 Jun.</p>
<p>A separate estimate released on Tuesday by the Congressional Budget Office put the total cost of the war higher, at $38bn, which includes replaced expended munitions and equipment lost in battle, increased flying hours and greater fuel costs.</p>
<p>US Patriot air defence systems, seen here at Raytheon headquarters in Massachusetts, have been heavily used in the Iran war.</p>
<p>Among the losses have been four F-15e fighter jets, a highly advanced F-35 Joint Strike Fighter and 30 MQ-9 drones destroyed in "various circumstances".</p>
<p>When US and Israeli strikes began, Trump repeatedly said that the conflict would be over within weeks.</p>
<p>More recently, he said the conflict might not end until "right after" the November midterm elections in the US.</p>
<p>Polls show that the war is unpopular among Americans.</p>
<p>One Reuters/Ipsos poll released in early September indicated that only 23% of Americans - and only half of Republicans - believe the US to be in a stronger position with Iran compared with before hostilities broke out.</p>
