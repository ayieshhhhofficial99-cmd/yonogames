<h2>Body</h2>
<p>Denmark has accused a Russian warship of firing two emergency flares at one of its helicopters while it was tracking the vessel in the Baltic Sea.</p>
<p>The Danish government has summoned the Russian ambassador over the incident, which it said happened during a "routine" monitoring operation on Monday.</p>
<p>Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen branded the Russian action "reckless" and said it was designed to "intimidate and divide".</p>
<p>Russia said it would "thoroughly investigate" the incident but accused Danish helicopters of making "dangerous manoeuvres" near Russian warships.</p>
<p>It came on the same day that Nato forces shot down a drone over Lithuania in the latest security scare on the alliance's eastern flank, where tensions remain high amid Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.</p>
<p>In a statement detailing the incident in the Baltic Sea, the Danish armed forces said: "One of the air force's Fennec helicopters was today fired at with flares during a routine photographic operation of a Russian frigate, which was located in international waters off Gedser."</p>
<p>Of the two flares fired at the helicopter, one passed close to it, the statement added.</p>
<p>The Danish foreign ministry described the incident as "completely unacceptable".</p>
<p>Danish Foreign Minister Lars Lokke Rasmussen accused Russia of "gradually shifting the boundary for what they consider acceptable behaviour", adding: "That is something we cannot accept."</p>
<p>In a statement, Russian ambassador Vladimir Barbin said Moscow would "thoroughly investigate all the circumstances of the incident" but accused the Danish helicopter of carrying out "provocative actions".</p>
<p>Barbin said this was "not the first time that Danish Air Force helicopters have carried out dangerous manoeuvres near Russian warships".</p>
<p>He said he had complained about a similar incident last year, adding that the latest event showed that "communication problems lie not with the Russian Navy's vessels, but with the Danish side".</p>
<p>Russia's behaviour was "reckless and dangerous," the President of the European Commission Ursula von der Leyen said in a post on social media.</p>
<p>This incident – together with the drone over Lithuania – was "part of a broader pattern of Russian aggression and provocation against Europe", she added.</p>
<p>Danish Defence Minister Jeppe Bruus told Reuters no attempt to make contact with helicopters had been made by the frigate's crew.</p>
<p>Overnight, Nato fighter pilots shot down a drone that entered Lithuania's airspace.</p>
<p>Authorities said the drone likely entered southern Lithuania, near the second-largest city of Kaunas, from neighbouring Belarus shortly after midnight on Tuesday.</p>
<p>The origins of the drone have yet to be determined, the Lithuanian national crisis management centre said, but the country's president pointed to increased Russian aggression in recent months.</p>
<p>President Gitanas Nausėda wrote on X: "With Russia intensifying its aggression against Ukraine, such readiness is vital for our region. Together with our Nato Allies, Lithuania will defend its airspace."</p>
<p>Lithuania shares a large part of its southern and eastern border with Belarus, a key Russian ally.</p>
<p>Poland also announced there had been "aviation operations" in its airspace on Monday night "due to the activity of Russian Federation's jet-powered unmanned aerial vehicles conducting strikes on Ukrainian territory".</p>
<p>It said the overnight operations were of a "preventive nature" and aimed at "securing airspace and its protection, especially in areas adjacent to threatened regions".</p>
<p>Nato jets have been scrambled several times to shoot down stray Ukrainian drones that have crossed into Baltic countries Estonia and Latvia this year.</p>
<p>Ukraine has blamed the stray drones on Russian electronic warfare, technology designed to change the flight paths of Ukrainian drones.</p>
