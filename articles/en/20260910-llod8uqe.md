<p>When a flight is cancelled or delayed, passengers affected have various rights – such as a refund or be put on another flight. However, these can vary depending on which carrier you are flying with and from where.</p>
<p>Only when an airline is at fault - not issues such as air traffic control problems - is extra compensation payable. Here's what it means for you.</p>
<p>When flights are delayed or cancelled, UK and EU airlines, and other carriers when you are departing a UK or EU airport, have a duty to look after you.</p>
<p>The reason for the delay or cancellation does not matter. So anyone affected by the air transport delays will be eligible.</p>
<p>That includes providing meals and accommodation, if necessary, and getting you to your destination. The airline should organise putting you on an alternative flight, at no extra cost.</p>
<p>Additional losses, such as unused accommodation, might require a claim to a credit card provider, if that was the payment option used.</p>
<p>After that, a claim may need to go to your travel insurance provider. But there is no standard definition of what is covered.</p>
<p>It may require a close look at the details of the policy to see what is covered, and in which circumstances.</p>
<p>Passengers are also being urged to heed travel advice from the UK government , external , as this can also affect travel insurance rights.</p>
<p>If your flight is covered by UK law, your airline must let you choose between either getting a refund or being booked on to an alternative flight.</p>
<p>That's regardless of how far in advance the cancellation was made.</p>
<p>You can get your money back for any part of the ticket you have not used.</p>
<p>So, if you booked a return flight and the outbound leg is cancelled, you can get the full cost of the return ticket refunded.</p>
<p>If you still want to travel, your airline must find you an alternative flight.</p>
<p>If another airline is flying to your destination significantly sooner, or there are other suitable modes of transport, then you have a right to be booked on to that alternative transport instead.</p>
<p>If your flight was coming into the UK on a non-UK or EU airline, then you should check the terms and conditions of your booking.</p>

<p>If you are stuck abroad or at the airport because of a flight cancellation, airlines covered by UK legislation must also provide you with other assistance.</p>
<p>a reasonable amount of food and drink (often in the form of vouchers)</p>
<p>a way for you to communicate (often by refunding the cost of calls)</p>
<p>free accommodation, if you have to stay overnight to fly the next day</p>
<p>If your airline is unable to arrange assistance, you have the right to organise this yourself and claim back the cost later.</p>
<p>The Civil Aviation Authority advises people to keep receipts and not spend more than necessary.</p>
<p>Civil Aviation Authority: Delays and cancellations , external</p>
<p>You are entitled to the same assistance as for a cancellation if your flight is delayed by more than two hours for a short-haul flight, three hours for a medium-haul, or four hours for a long-haul.</p>
<p>If you are delayed by more than five hours and no longer want to travel, you can get a full refund.</p>
<p>If you booked a package holiday with a company that is an ABTA member and your flight is cancelled, you are entitled to a suitable alternative flight or a full refund.</p>
<p>Disruption caused by things like air traffic control issues – but also bad weather, strikes by airport staff, or conflict, or other "extraordinary circumstances" – does not entitle you to extra compensation.</p>
<p>However, in other circumstances, when it is considered to be the airline's fault, you have a number of rights under UK law , external .</p>
<p>These apply as long as you are flying from a UK airport on any airline, arriving at a UK airport on an EU or UK airline, or arriving at an airport in the EU on a UK airline.</p>
<p>What you are entitled to depends on what caused the cancellation and how much notice you are given.</p>
<p>If your flight is cancelled with less than two weeks' notice, you may be able to claim compensation based on the timings of the alternative flight you are offered.</p>
<p>The amount you are entitled to also depends on how far you were travelling:</p>
<p>for flights under 1,500km , such as Glasgow to Amsterdam, you can claim up to £220 per person</p>
<p>for flights of 1,500km to 3,500km , such as East Midlands to Marrakesh, you can claim up to £350 per person</p>
<p>for flights over 3,500km , such as London to New York, you can claim up to £520 per person</p>
<p>Airlines will not refund you for loss of earnings. Travel insurance policies will not usually cover loss of earnings either.</p>
<p>If you think you're going to be late back at work because of flight delays, you have a responsibility to let your employer know, legal experts say.</p>
<p>You should agree with your employer how to deal with the absence – for example, by using annual leave or taking unpaid leave.</p>
<p>Employers have no legal obligation to pay employees who are absent in this situation, experts say, unless it is stated in their contract.</p>
