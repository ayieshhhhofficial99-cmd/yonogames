<h2>Body</h2>
<p>Mount Fuji's official climbing season runs from July to early September</p>
<p>From next year, people who attempt to climb Mount Fuji during off-peak seasons and then require rescue by helicopter will have to pay for the service, authorities governing the northern part of the peak say.</p>
<p>The fee is among a series of measures Japan's Yamanashi prefecture is introducing to deter reckless and poorly prepared climbers.</p>
<p>A similar fee of 8,000 yen ($51; £38) per five minutes is already in place in nearby Saitama prefecture for other mountainous areas and will be used as a reference.</p>
<p>Yamanashi authorities will also require those looking to scale Fuji during the off-season, which runs from early September to July the following year, to submit detailed climbing plans.</p>
<p>"We ask the general public to refrain from climbing during the off-season. We hope that these measures will cause people to rethink what climbing Mount Fuji during the winter is like," Yamanashi Governor Kotaro Nagasaki said on Monday.</p>
<p>Officials in Yamanashi and neighbouring Shizuoka prefecture, which governs the southern part of Fuji, have seen a rise in off-season and unplanned climbs, some of which have left climbers stranded.</p>
<p>Shizuoka authorities say they will impose penalties on people who fail to register their off-season climbing attempts and strengthen efforts to keep climbers out of restricted areas.</p>
<p>They did not announce a helicopter rescue fee.</p>
<p>Mount Fuji's official climbing season runs from July to early September, with about 200,000 hikers attempting the summit each year.</p>
<p>Climbing outside of those months is highly discouraged although permitted up to the fifth of ten stations.</p>
<p>However, most mountain huts and toilet facilities are closed off season. The weather and trail conditions also tend to be more severe.</p>
