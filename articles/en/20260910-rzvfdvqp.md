<h2>Body</h2>
<p>The White House has taken down a Tetris-style game on its website called "Build The Wall", days after The Tetris Company raised concerns over its use of the game's likeness.</p>
<p>The game, which saw players stack blocks to build a wall to "protect the border" is one of five arcade games which have appeared on the White House website.</p>
<p>On 4 September the official Tetris account on X posted , external the firm was "not involved in the creation" of the game and it took "copyright infringement very seriously".</p>
<p>The White House has not responded to requests for comment from the BBC or given an official reason for the takedown.</p>
<p>"At Tetris we believe in the power of connection and building people together, not dividing them," the firm said in its initial response to the game.</p>
<p>"To our fans everywhere: we love you, we see you, and we're grateful to have you in our community."</p>
<p>The Tetris Company has had previous success in suing firms for infringing on its copyright - settling for an undisclosed fee with the makers of the iPhone game Mino in 2012, which it claimed stole the look and feel of the block-builder.</p>
<p>Four other arcade games still remain on the mini-game section of the White House website.</p>
<p>"Flappy Bill", a clone of the 2013 viral hit Flappy Bird sees players fly a bald eagle and dodging monuments from the National Mall.</p>
<p>Meanwhile in Rio Run, players must "patrol the line along the river", with gameplay reminiscent of the classic phone game Snake.</p>
<p>In Rio Run, players must "patrol the line along the river" in a Snake-style minigame</p>
<p>When players run into the edge of the map, a "Deported" number shows how many people the player was able to catch.</p>
<p>The games have drawn criticism from politicians, with Connecticut Democratic state senator Matt Lesser tweeting , external : "Heating oil is near an all-time high but hey you can play Border Tsar Tom Homan in a video game".</p>
<p>The arcade section is just one example of how the Trump administration has increasingly incorporated video game culture into its political messaging on social media.</p>
<p>In October 2025, the official White House X account drew criticism for posting an AI-generated picture of President Trump , external in a suit from the video game Halo.</p>
<p>Meanwhile on 6 March it posted a video which mixed footage of US military strikes in Iran with film and TV clips, as well a clip from Halo's protagonist Master Chief saying "finishing this fight".</p>
<p>The post was condemned by the Master Chief voice actor Steve Downes in a now-deleted tweet which called for "the producers of this disgusting and juvenile war porn" to remove his voice from the video.</p>
<p>The Pokémon Company International also criticised the White House's use of its imagery in March, when it included a tiny version of the popular character Pikachu in a meme posted online with the phrase "Make America Great Again".</p>
