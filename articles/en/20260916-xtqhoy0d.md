<h2>Body</h2>
<p>4 min read Oct 12, 2023 Updated Feb 28, 2026</p>
<p>If you look purely at their form, Apple’s products are all rounded rectangular</p>
<p>slabs. Make the slab as large and thin as an open coffee-table book, and you get</p>
<p>a Studio Display. Make it tall and hand-held, and you get an iPhone. Make it an</p>
<p>even smaller and thicker, and you get an AirPods case.</p>
<p>The physical dimensions are dictated first by the human body. An iPhone or</p>
<p>AirPods case must be hand-holdable by the majority of buyers. Dimensions are</p>
<p>further dictated by components inside. A display (as far as it remains rigid and</p>
<p>not bendable) can never get smaller than the panel.</p>
<p>Rounded corners are less likely to cut a person or other object. They are also</p>
<p>more durable. Imagine dropping an iPhone with a sharp corner. It’s more likely</p>
<p>to suffer a sever dent or crack versus one with a rounded corner.</p>
<p>Rounded objects are also seen more favorably. Universal Principles of Design explains the Contour Bias phenomenon :</p>
<p>“When presented with objects that posses sharp angles or pointed features, a</p>
<p>region of the human brain involved in fear processing, the amygdala, is</p>
<p>activated”</p>
<p>It’s important to state that the round corners in Apple’s products are not</p>
<p>circular, but instead “squircles” .</p>
<p>Squircles are complex curves that remove the discontinuity between the straight</p>
<p>and curved sections of a surface. Look at a curved surface that isn’t a squircle</p>
<p>and you’ll be able to see where the straight line ends and the curve starts.</p>
<p>Naoto Fukasawa, in a talk I attended years ago ,</p>
<p>showed us the following graphic when explaining the overarching philosophy</p>
<p>behind his designs. He observed that the closer an object is to the human, the</p>
<p>rounder it gets.</p>
<p>He followed this principle in how he designed appliances both large and small</p>
<p>for Muji.</p>
<p>It didn’t immediately click at the talk, but I later realized that Apple follows</p>
<p>the same idea. The small objects that are closest to our body — Apple Watch,</p>
<p>AirPods case, Apple Pencil — are quite round. The ones furthest — desktop</p>
<p>computers, displays — are slightly rounded, if at all.</p>
<p>Intuitively, it makes sense. I find myself drawn to pick up and fidget with the</p>
<p>AirPods case as if it were a pebble. However, with a display, I’m content with</p>
<p>viewing it from the distance. There are ergonomic concerns as well. An Apple</p>
<p>Watch with flat sides wouldn’t be comfortable as the edges could dig into the</p>
<p>wrist.</p>
<p>Naturally, the next question to ask is “how round should a product be?</p>
<p>”. Is it</p>
<p>purely subjective, or is there a formula?</p>
<p>I found images of all major Apple products and started measuring. I first</p>
<p>decided what the “front” of an object is. For a device with a display, the</p>
<p>display-side is the front. For a desktop computer, it’s the part that faces</p>
<p>forward on a desk.</p>
<p>I then measured the smaller side of the rectangle and the diameter of the curve</p>
<p>(two times the radius).</p>
<p>By dividing the diameter by the length of the side, I get a roundness score in</p>
<p>the form of a percentage between zero and one hundred.</p>
<p>After calculating the scores, a clear pattern had emerged.</p>
<p>Desktop-style devices at 0%. Displays are between 2% and 3%. MacBooks are</p>
<p>between 7% and 10%. iPads are between 12% and 18%. iPhones are around 33%.</p>
<p>Personal objects like AirPods and Apple Watch, have a score right around 50%.</p>
<p>AirPods Max is 88%, but that number is likely dictated by ergonomics.</p>
<p>Objects closest to people, like the Apple Pencil and AirPod earbuds are 100%.</p>
<p>I’ve included the full list of roundness scores at the end of this post .</p>
<p>Every product category follows the “closer to the human, the rounder the object”</p>
<p>pattern, and each product neatly sticks close to the rest of its category.</p>
<p>Look around you. Where else do you see this phenomenon?</p>
<p>Are there areas where</p>
<p>the pattern breaks?</p>
<p>How do these exceptions make you feel?</p>
<p>Keep a look out as Apple continues to release new products. They will likely</p>
<p>continue to stick to this formula. We see this with the impending Apple Vision</p>
<p>Pro, which is extremely curved in nearly every dimension.</p>
